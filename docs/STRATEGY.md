# Why this direction

The supplied dashboard rewards technology, business value, originality and presentation. It also lists several industrial fault/maintenance copilots already submitted. Another cited-manual chatbot would be difficult to distinguish.

## The differentiator

**Evidence → competing explanations → next informative observation → constrained alternatives → accountable action.**

The strongest stage moment is a judge removing a part, contradicting a sensor or changing a deadline—and seeing an attractive action become impossible. The interface explains why.

Industrial context supplies an understandable user and quantifiable tradeoffs. The product is now a decision workbench rather than autonomous maintenance. Pressure, thermal quality and energy demonstrate the pattern without pretending to support arbitrary machines.

## Alternatives considered

| Direction | Attraction | Reason not to pivot with hours remaining |
|---|---|---|
| General document/RAG assistant | Easy integration | Weak differentiation; retrieval alone does not demonstrate decision value |
| Industrial computer vision | Visually immediate | No real labeled images or independent validation supplied |
| Healthcare agent | Important human-review need | No validated workflow/data; safety/privacy preparation exceeds remaining time |
| Generative audience experience | Visual potential | Discards useful implementation and weakens measurable operator outcome |
| Evidence-first industrial decisions | Interactive, testable, inspectable cost | Best reuse and demonstrable differentiator; sponsor verification still essential |

## Innovation and limits

- Product contribution: users challenge inputs, inspect discriminating observations, and compare eligible actions before approval.
- Engineering contribution: source-grounded extraction reaches planning; model output is bounded to actual evidence and feasible plans.
- Not a research claim: weights, likelihoods, costs and thresholds are authored assumptions, not learned physics or calibrated industrial risk.
- Not a novelty claim: information gain, constraints and human approval are established techniques. The integrated experience differentiates this prototype.

## Most valuable remaining proof

1. A genuine AMD inference receipt with actual model and returned usage.
2. Actual Evolus library/extractor calls, followed by one human-approved sandbox ticket.
3. A cold-input example changing CSV/reference fields rather than case titles.
4. Measured operator time-to-reviewed-decision versus usual workflow; separate assistance from realized production savings.
5. Independent fault-labeled cases and calibration. The 44 local checks are contracts, not that study.

A credible competitive prototype is possible; a polished offline presentation cannot replace mandatory AMD usage. No deck or implementation guarantees a prize.
